### 🔹 Question 41 (1.9.2)
You have been tasked with performing a risk assessment using the "loss expectancy" model on the organization's laptop computers as there seems to be a high failure rate. You use the formula ALE (Annual Loss Expectancy) = SLE (Single Loss Expectancy) x ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence), with the SLE being calculated by multiplying the AV (Asset Value) by the EF (exposure factor).

AV- $1250.00 EF- 33% (the cost to replace the CPU) ARO- CPU burnout every 9 months

Your laptop supplier is offering a support and maintenance contract for $600 per annum, per laptop, which includes parts and labor. Calculate the ALE. Is the support contract cost-effective?
<br/>A. Given the ALE and assuming a single failure: yes, it is.
<br/>B. Given the ALE and assuming a single failure: no, it is not.
<br/>C. Given the ALE and assuming multiple failures: yes, it is.
<br/>D. Given the ALE and assuming multiple failures: no, it is not.

**✅ Correct Answer: D. Given the ALE and assuming multiple failures: no, it is not.**

```text
Feedback:
SLE = AV × EF = $1250 × 0.33 = $412.50
ARO = every 9 months = 12/9 = 1.333 occurrences per year
ALE = SLE × ARO = $412.50 × 1.333 = $550 per laptop per year

The support contract costs $600 per year, which is $50 more than the ALE.

Under a single-failure assumption (ARO = 1), ALE = $412.50, making the contract $187.50 more expensive.
Under multiple failures (ARO = 1.333), ALE = $550, making the contract $50 more expensive.

In both cases, the contract is NOT cost-effective. Since the scenario implies ongoing risk
(CPU burnout every 9 months), multiple failures should be assumed. Therefore, Answer D is correct.
```
