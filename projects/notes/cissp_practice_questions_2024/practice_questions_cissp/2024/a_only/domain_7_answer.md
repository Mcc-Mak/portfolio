# Domain 7 - Security Operations - Answer Keys

## Question 191 (7.1.2)
**Correct Answer:** D. Suspending the rights of individuals and other parties during the investigation process, particularly with respect to privacy, self-incrimination, fair employment, and appropriate working conditions

**Feedback:** In most jurisdictions, anyone suspected of a criminal act is presumed innocent until proven guilty. This means that the rights of individuals and other parties must be protected.

## Question 192 (7.1.2)
**Correct Answer:** D. Temporarily suspending certain employee rights, such as privacy in communications and personal data access, without obtaining proper authorization or following due legal processes.

**Feedback:** In most jurisdictions, employees retain certain rights even during investigations, and violating these rights can compromise the investigation and lead to legal repercussions.

## Question 195 (7.1.5)
**Correct Answer:** B. Timestamps of file modifications on the suspect's computer

**Feedback:** Timestamps of file modifications on the suspect's computer are crucial digital artifacts in a forensic investigation. They can provide insights into the activities of the suspect, such as when specific files were accessed or altered, which could be critical in establishing a timeline of the alleged espionage activities.

## Question 196 (7.2.1)

## Question 199 (7.2.6)

**Feedback:** An increase in political tension might be a precursor to violence, and an alarm activation might be an indication of a break-in.

## Question 200 (7.2.7)

**Feedback:** Information learned through a threat intelligence process will likely be fed into both risk management and the business impact analysis.

## Question 203 (7.6.2)

## Question 204 (7.6.2)
**Correct Answer:** A. Collection

**Feedback:** Forensic investigations are carried out by specialists who are trained in the use of the appropriate tools and techniques. It is during the collection phase where the incident response team may become involved—securing the scene, identifying potential sources of evidence, etc.

## Question 206 (7.6.2)
**Correct Answer:** D. Following a predefined protocol that begins with securing the suspected employee's computer, then collecting, examining, and analyzing data from the computer and network logs; and finally documenting any findings

**Feedback:** This option best aligns with the NIST forensic cycle. It begins with the preparation phase (securing the computer), followed by the collection of relevant data from the computer and network logs. The examination and analysis of this data are then conducted in a structured manner.

## Question 210 (7.2.1, 7.7.2)
**Correct Answer:** B. Signature matching

**Feedback:** Signature matching looks for known attack patterns.

## Question 211 (7.4.1)
**Correct Answer:** B. Need to know

**Feedback:** An individual should not have access to information unless their job description requires it. The need to know can be viewed as one aspect of least privilege. Level of clearance is not a reason for accessing information.

## Question 214 (7.10.1)
**Correct Answer:** A. Store one copy of the data on the company's local server, another copy on an external hard drive kept in the office, and a third copy on cloud storage.

**Feedback:** This approach aligns well with the 3-2-1 strategy. It involves three copies of the data (one on the local server, one on an external hard drive, and one in the cloud), two types of media (local server and external hard drive), and one off-site copy (cloud storage).

## Question 215 (7.10.1, 7.10.4)
**Correct Answer:** C. A type of snapshotting

**Feedback:** A snapshot occurs in virtualized storage environments and takes advantage of the environment to capture mirrors of the data blocks on the media without disrupting production operations. With CDP, every time a data block changes, the block is snapshotted.

## Question 218 (7.10.2)
**Correct Answer:** B. Joint operating agreement (JOA)

**Feedback:** A joint operating agreement (JOA) and/or memorandum of understanding (MOU) are contractual techniques used by governmental organizations for sharing operating locations if a partner's environment is disrupted.

## Question 219 (7.10.4)
**Correct Answer:** D. RAID

**Feedback:** Redundant Array of Independent Disks (RAID) provides a failover solution to protect data in the event of a single disk failure.
